# Muhammad Ali v. Respondent No. 1 (CRL.P. 207-K/2025)

Supreme Court of Pakistan | CRL.P. 207-K/2025 | bench: Justice Muhammad Ali Mazhar, Justice Irfan Saadat Khan

## Facts

The petitioner sought the cancellation of pre-arrest bail granted to Respondent No. 1 by the High Court of Sindh in connection with FIR No. 03/2025, which involves allegations of negligence leading to electrocution. The complainant alleged that the respondent and other WAPDA officials supplied electricity through an unauthorized line that broke and electrocuted the complainant's brother. The FIR was lodged with a nine-month delay, and the complainant admitted during the proceedings that he was also purchasing electricity through a contractor rather than an authorized utility provider.

- *CRL.P. 207-K/2025, para p0*: "By means of this Criminal Petition, the petitioner has sought the cancellation of bail granted to Respondent No.1 by the High Court of Sindh, Circuit Court, Larkana vide order dated 20.10.2025"
- *CRL.P. 207-K/2025, para 2*: "the complainant, his brother Noor Hussain, his son Ali Raza and his nephew Ihsan Ali were standing and saw that PVC wire broke/unplugged which converged with high tension wire and electrocuted the complainant's brother Noor Husain."
- *CRL.P. 207-K/2025, para 4*: "The learned Addl. P.G could not controvert the delay of nine months in lodging FIR"
- *CRL.P. 207-K/2025, para 9*: "When we categorically asked the complainant how he was energizing his shop, he informed that the respondent No.1 is performing as a contractor with collusion of WAPDA Officials and supplying electricity to the shop of complainant against some monetary consideration."

## Issues

Whether the High Court's order granting bail to the respondent was legally flawed, and whether there are sufficient grounds to warrant the cancellation of that bail under Section 497(5) of the Cr.P.C.

- *CRL.P. 207-K/2025, para 7*: "The yardstick for the cancellation of bail under Section 497 (5) of the Cr.P.C are characterized into two fundamental scaffoldings such as that the bail was granted in violation of mandatory statutory provisions or established legal principles"

## Holding

The Supreme Court declined to interfere with the High Court's bail order, noting that the case requires further inquiry and that the petitioner ultimately chose not to press the petition.

- *CRL.P. 207-K/2025, para 11*: "As a result of above discussion, the bail granting order passed by the learned High Court does not require any interference. Since the petitioner himself conceded and is not inclined to press this petition moved for cancellation of bail, therefore this criminal petition is disposed of accordingly."

## Reasoning

The High Court correctly identified that the nine-month delay in lodging the FIR and the nature of the allegations against the respondent necessitated further inquiry. The Supreme Court emphasized that bail cancellation requires proof of illegal exercise of discretion, misuse of liberty, or interference with the judicial process, none of which were established here. Furthermore, the complainant's own admission regarding his involvement in the unauthorized purchase of electricity undermined his position, and the court noted that the guilt of the accused can only be determined through a full-dress trial.

- *CRL.P. 207-K/2025, para 7*: "By and large, this Court does not interfere with bail granting orders of the High Court unless the discretion exercised by the High Court seems to be fanciful, arbitrary, or outside the precise bounds established by dint of judicial precedents"

## Outcome

The criminal petition for the cancellation of bail was disposed of as not pressed by the petitioner.

- *CRL.P. 207-K/2025, para 11*: "Since the petitioner himself conceded and is not inclined to press this petition moved for cancellation of bail, therefore this criminal petition is disposed of accordingly."
