# Contract comparison (DRAFT — for lawyer review)

Old: IT Services and Supply Agreement (version 1, sent by the Client)  
New: IT Services and Supply Agreement (version 2, Contractor's markup)  
Client: Pak Telecom Enterprises (Pvt) Ltd (the Client). 9 changes, 1 clauses unchanged.

| # | Clause | Change | Risk | Favours | Legal impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 Services and Scope of Work | changed: Stylistic change to the performance obligation language. | none | neither | This is a non-substantive change that does not alter the legal obligations of the parties. |
| 2 | 3 Term and Renewal | changed: Introduction of automatic three-year renewals with a short 15-day notice period. | medium | Contractor | The short notice period increases the risk of being locked into a long-term contract unintentionally. This deviates from the playbook preference for a 30-90 day notice period. |
| 3 | 4 Payment Terms and Withholding Tax | changed: Extended payment terms and introduction of high interest on late payments. | medium | Contractor | The 60-day payment term is less favourable than the 30-45 day standard. The 2% monthly interest (24% annually) may be viewed as a penalty under Section 74 of the Contract Act, 1872, if it is deemed excessive. |
| 4 | 5 Confidentiality | changed: Permits disclosure to subcontractors/affiliates and reduces the survival period to two years. | low | Contractor | Allowing disclosure to subcontractors is a standard commercial practice, but the reduction in the survival period from seven to two years provides less long-term protection for sensitive data. |
| 5 | 6 Indemnity | changed: Makes indemnity mutual and adds a broad indemnity from the Client for use of deliverables. | high | Contractor | The new indemnity for the Client's use of deliverables is overly broad and potentially exposes the Client to liability for the Contractor's own failures. This deviates from the playbook requirement for mutual, limited third-party claim indemnities. |
| 6 | 7 Limitation of Liability | changed: Drastically reduces the Contractor's liability cap and removes the Client's reciprocal protection. | high | Contractor | Reducing the cap to one month's fees is highly restrictive and leaves the Client with insufficient recourse. Removing the Client's liability cap while keeping the Contractor's cap low is a significant imbalance. |
| 7 | 8 Termination | changed: Extends the cure period for breach and grants the Contractor a right to terminate for convenience. | high | Contractor | The 90-day cure period is excessively long, and granting the Contractor a right to terminate for convenience while potentially limiting the Client's rights creates a significant operational risk. |
| 8 | 9 Non-Competition | changed: Replaces a non-solicitation clause with a broad non-compete restriction. | high | Contractor | Under Section 27 of the Contract Act, 1872, this restraint on trade is likely void and unenforceable as it prevents the Client from engaging other providers without justification. |
| 9 | 10 Governing Law and Jurisdiction | changed: Changes the dispute resolution forum from local arbitration to foreign courts. | high | Contractor | Moving jurisdiction to Dubai courts significantly increases the cost and complexity of enforcing the contract. This violates the playbook requirement for local dispute resolution in Pakistan. |
