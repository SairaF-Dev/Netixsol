# Student guide

Ilm Academy teaches Python one concept at a time. It works out what you already know, skips
what you have mastered and gives you practice at the right level. When you are stuck, a tutor
helps you find the answer yourself.

## Logging in

Open the link your teacher gave you and log in with your username and password. You stay logged
in for 8 hours. On a phone, the menu is at the bottom of the screen.

## Today

This is your starting page.

- **The heading** shows how many days your plan takes at your daily study time.
- **Next step** is the concept to learn now. Press it to start.
- **Quick checks · you probably know these** are concepts the platform thinks you already know.
  A few questions confirm it, so you don't spend time re-learning them.
- **Your plan in UrduLish** is the same plan, explained in Roman Urdu.

## Path

Your whole route through the course, in order:

- what you have **mastered**;
- what is **ready** (its prerequisites are done);
- what comes later and **after** which concept;
- time estimates for each concept;
- the lecture that teaches each concept.

**Mastered** means you got the last 3 questions on it right and the platform is at least 95%
sure you know it. If you then miss questions, the concept can come back for review.

## Practising a concept

Press a concept, then **Start questions**.

1. Answer the multiple-choice or short-answer question and press **Check**.
2. You see at once whether you were right.
   - **A wrong answer** also tells you the common mistake behind it, and for code questions
     what the program **really prints**.
   - **Your mastery** of the concept updates after every answer.
3. Press **Next question**. Questions you missed come back later.

When the concept has a coding exercise, **Try the coding exercise** takes you there.

## Coding exercises

Open **Practice** to see all exercises, or follow the link from a concept.

1. Read the task. **Task rule** shows any rule, for example "do not use `max()`". Breaking it
   costs marks even if the tests pass.
2. Write your function in the editor. The **Example tests** show some of the calls the grader
   tries.
3. Press **Run tests**. Your code runs in a safe sandbox with no internet and a 10-second limit,
   so a mistake can't break anything. The result usually takes 1-2 seconds.
4. The result shows:
   - **your mark out of 10**;
   - **every example test that failed**, with what was expected and what your code returned;
   - **how many hidden tests failed and how** (wrong value, error, too slow). The hidden tests
     themselves stay secret, so the mark shows whether you solved the problem, not whether you
     matched the examples;
   - **Next step**: the one most useful thing to fix first;
   - **Code quality** tips, and a link to the lecture on the concept.
5. Fix your code and run again. **Your recent runs** keeps your last 5.

You can run up to 12 times a minute. If you see "That's a lot of runs in one minute", wait a
moment.

**Messages you may see:**

| message | what it means |
|---|---|
| "Your code is taking a long time. Is there a loop that never ends?" | your code did not finish in time; check your loop conditions |
| "Correct, but too slow on a large input." | the answer is right, but the method is slow for big inputs |
| "Your code's output looked like a test result, so this run goes to your teacher to check." | your program printed something that looks like the grader's own output. A teacher looks at it. Nothing happens automatically |

## The tutor

Press **Ask the tutor** (on a phone, **Ask**) on any page.

- **Ask anything about the course**, in English or UrduLish, for example "print aur return mein
  kya farq hai?". The answer comes from the course lectures, with links to the lesson.
- **On an exercise, press Get a hint.** Each further press goes one step further:
  1. a question that helps you notice the problem;
  2. the concept you need;
  3. a partial hint pointing at your code;
  4. a worked example of a **different** problem using the same idea.

  You get 4 hints per exercise. The tutor **never gives the solution**, even if asked. That is
  on purpose: you learn by finding it.
- You can choose English or UrduLish, or let the tutor detect your language.
- Limits: 8 requests a minute and 80 a day.
- If you see "the tutor is unavailable", the AI service is busy or its daily limit is used up.
  Try later, or use the lesson links.
- **Privacy:** what you type to the tutor is not stored. Only counts are kept, such as how many
  hints were used.

## Your marks and copying

Every mark comes from tests plus clear rules. Low marks, and runs that look unusual, go to your
teacher to check: a teacher can raise a mark. If two programs look very similar, a teacher
reviews them side by side. **Nothing is decided against you automatically.**
