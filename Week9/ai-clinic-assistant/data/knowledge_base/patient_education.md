# Patient Health Education Leaflets

## 1. Dengue Fever Awareness & Care
* **Symptoms**: Sudden high fever (104°F), severe headache, pain behind eyes, joint/muscle pain ("breakbone fever"), nausea, skin rash.
* **Warning Signs (Seek Emergency)**: Severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, bleeding gums/nose, extreme fatigue, difficulty breathing.
* **Home Management**: Stay well hydrated (ORS, coconut water, fresh juices). Take Paracetamol (Panadol) for fever.
* **STRICT WARNING**: DO NOT take Aspirin, Ibuprofen, Brufen, or Disprin as they increase bleeding risks in Dengue.

## 2. Diabetes Self-Management
* **Blood Sugar Targets**: Fasting < 100 mg/dL; 2-hour post-meal < 140 mg/dL.
* **Dietary Guidance**: Reduce refined sugars, white rice, white bread, and sweetened drinks. Increase fiber intake (whole grains, vegetables, lentils).
* **Hypoglycemia Signs (Low Blood Sugar < 70 mg/dL)**: Sweating, trembling, dizziness, fast heartbeat, confusion.
* **Immediate Action for Low Sugar**: Consume 15g fast-acting sugar immediately (3-4 glucose tablets, half glass fruit juice, or 1 tablespoon sugar/honey). Recheck blood sugar after 15 minutes.

## 3. High Blood Pressure (Hypertension) Management
* **Target Reading**: Below 120/80 mmHg.
* **Lifestyle Modification**: Reduce daily sodium/salt intake (< 5g per day). Avoid processed foods, pickles, and fried snacks.
* **Exercise**: 30 minutes of moderate aerobic exercise (brisk walking) 5 days a week.
* **Medication Adherence**: Never stop prescribed blood pressure medication abruptly, even if feeling completely healthy.

## 4. Infant Fever Management (< 5 years)
* **Definition of Fever**: Body temperature 100.4°F (38°C) or higher.
* **Red Flag (< 3 months old)**: Any fever in an infant under 3 months is a medical emergency requiring immediate doctor evaluation.
* **General Measures**: Keep child lightly dressed. Offer plenty of fluids (breastmilk, water). Use Paracetamol pediatric drops/syrup based strictly on weight, not age.
* **Avoid**: Cold sponge baths, alcohol rubs, or giving Aspirin to children.
